# Biological context and interpretation

The studies below place the analyzed expression patterns in biological context. Bracketed numbers refer to the stable package bibliography in `References_verified.txt`; these identifiers are independent of the revised manuscript numbering.

## Mitochondrial priming

Mitochondrial apoptotic competence can influence the susceptibility of cancer cells to CAR-T-mediated killing [8]. Related work in NK-cell immunotherapy also links target-cell susceptibility to mitochondrial apoptosis [9]. BH3 profiling studies established the concept of mitochondrial priming in thymocytes and associated pretreatment tumor priming with chemotherapy response [10,11]. These observations support a role for the target cell's apoptotic state without treating evidence from these different systems as interchangeable.

## Public resources

We used DepMap and CCLE expression data to place the six experimental models in a broader context of cytokine sensing and cell-death-related gene expression (Ghandi et al., 2019). Selected compound sensitivities were examined using CTRPv2 (Basu et al., 2013; Seashore-Ludlow et al., 2015; Rees et al., 2016). These analyses characterize expression and drug-response patterns; they do not measure genetic dependency or establish pathway activity.

## FAS and p53

The expression differences between HeLa and H460 identify candidate routes of resistance but do not establish their genetic or epigenetic causes. In genetically engineered mouse pancreatic cancer models, elimination of oncogenic KRAS restored FAS-associated immune susceptibility [37]. FAS signaling can also contribute to antigen-specific and bystander T-cell killing [38]. These findings motivate further investigation of FAS regulation in H460, but they do not demonstrate KRAS-dependent FAS promoter methylation in the present cells. Earlier work established p53-dependent regulation of CD95/FAS [40]. A separate study reported that combined actinomycin D and nutlin-3a treatment sensitized cancer cells, including NCI-H460, to FAS-ligand-mediated death [41]. Whether this approach improves CAR-T-mediated killing in the present experimental setting remains to be tested.

## cytokine responses

The bulk expression summaries show an overlapping inflammatory response to TNF+IFNγ and T-cell co-culture, including positive enrichment of the three selected Hallmark programs. Differences in threshold-defined gene-list membership do not establish a direct difference between the treatments. In particular, enrichment of lymphocyte-related terms in a co-culture-derived list cannot be assigned solely to tumor cells from these processed summaries. Persistent interferon signaling has been linked to resistance programs in other experimental settings [43], supporting the distinction between inflammatory transcription and effective tumor-cell death.

## NLRP3 and pyroptosis

NLRP3 overexpression alone did not restore the measured killing phenotype under the tested conditions. This observation does not identify which downstream component, if any, limits pyroptotic execution in these cells. Prior studies established that GSDME can connect caspase-3 activity to pyroptosis and contribute to antitumor immunity [48,49]. Those findings provide broader context, but they do not demonstrate a defect in PYCARD/ASC, inflammatory caspases or gasdermin function in the present HeLa models.
